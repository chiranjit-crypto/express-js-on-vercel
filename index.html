Source: CoRE Stack (IIT Delhi ICTD / Gram Vaani) land-use classification and village boundary geometry, plus each landscape's own village list. Gross cropped area is derived as net sown area × cropping intensity (CoRE Stack's own satellite classification), not a directly measured figure — treat it, like cropping intensity, as satellite-relative rather than audited. Contiguity is computed by testing whether each landscape's own villages share a border with each other inside their full administrative block; villages outside the landscape are shown for context only and are not tested. Patharpratima and East Khasi Hills have no confirmed village-level GIS boundaries in CoRE Stack, so contiguity cannot be assessed for them and their layout is schematic (sized by area, not true shape or position). Clicking a village now also shows a predominant land-cover tag (forest, cultivable, water, built-up, or mixed), computed from CoRE Stack's own cultivable/forest/water/built-up shares for that boundary — nothing is shown on the map itself until you click a boundary. This is a land-cover reading from satellite imagery, not terrain. CoRE Stack does carry terrain and elevation data (slope/plain/ridge/valley shares and min/max/mean elevation), but only at the micro-watershed (block-wide) level — it isn't matched into the per-village figures shown here, except for the “other villages” estimates, which draw on it directly. In Patratu, Rajnagar, Dantewada and Dharashiv, the further villages in each block that CoRE Stack hasn't classified are now shown directly on the map itself, coloured by their estimated dominant land cover and clickable for the same estimate detail — these are marked as estimates throughout, not CoRE Stack's own village-level figures. In Mau and Vempalli, other villages are shown with their real boundary and area only, since the watershed-level dataset needed for a land-cover estimate wasn't available for those two blocks.
